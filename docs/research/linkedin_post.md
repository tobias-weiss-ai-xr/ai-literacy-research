We analyzed 11,640 AI-literacy papers. The field isn't short on research
anymore — it's short on the *right* research.

The single thinnest, fastest-growing topic in the whole corpus is
`compliance/evaluation`: just 5 papers, up 300% year-on-year.

Translation: since the EU AI Act made AI literacy a duty under Article 4,
almost no one has published evidence that a compliance-readiness program
**actually worked**.

That's the gap worth filling — and the opening worth owning.

The good part: we no longer need to build measurement from scratch. Validated
instruments (AICOS, MAIL-CS, the new SFAILQ) exist. The move is to pair them
with *task-based* checks — measuring what people can do with AI, not what they
claim — and keep a per-role evidence record.

Organizations that train → measure → document will have the defensible
compliance story everyone else is quietly missing.
