**Subject: 11,640 papers on AI literacy — and the one gap that matters for you**

We just re-ran our living review of the AI-literacy literature. It now holds
**11,640 papers** across 20 categories — the field is no longer short on
writing. It is short on the *right* writing.

When we rank each topic cell by how under-supplied it is *relative to its
category*, and weight that by how fast it's growing, one white space dominates:
**evaluation** — and specifically, *proving an AI-literacy program actually
worked*.

The thinnest, fastest-growing cell in the whole corpus is
`compliance/evaluation`: just **5 papers, up 300% year-on-year**. In plain
terms: since the EU AI Act's Article 4 made "did our people get literate?" a
*compliance* question, almost nobody has published evidence that a compliance
readiness program succeeded.

**Why you should care.** Article 4 says staff must have AI literacy
"appropriate to their context." That means role-specific — not a generic
webinar. And "appropriate" is increasingly something you'll need to *show*, not
just claim.

**The good news.** The measurement tools finally exist — validated instruments
like AICOS, MAIL-CS and the new multi-cohort SFAILQ mean you don't build your
own. And a task-based check (not a smile-sheet) tells you what people can
actually *do* with AI, which is what matters.

**Our take.** The organizations that move first on *measured* AI literacy —
trained, then assessed against real tasks, then documented per role — will own
the defensible compliance story the rest of the market is silently missing.
